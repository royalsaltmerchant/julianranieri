Classic ghost directory / Seriko

Seriko せりこ

A foundational early character featuring the low-energy Seriko and her tiny companion Maruchii. Beginning as a Nise-Haruna skin before becoming a full ghost, Seriko became especially influential through her animated artwork: the Ukagaka shell-animation specification took its name from her.

Technical context

Seriko first appeared as a skin for Nise-Haruna on 2000-12-20 and later developed into a complete ghost. Her animated second shell was described in contemporary coverage as the first animated ghost shell in the scene. The name SERIKO was subsequently adopted for Ukagaka's general shell-animation specification. The preserved 2004 package identifies both the ghost and shell creator as MIY.

Sources

  1. The History of Ukagaka - Nise-Haruna chronology archived history

    Mirror of a 2002 chronology dating EXTRA STAGE's first Seriko skin to 2000-12-20 and documenting the January 2001 animation implementation associated with her.

  2. 「何か」システム用語集 “redo” 版 contemporary reference

    Contemporary terminology reference defines SERIKO as a general animation and dress-up drawing specification and explains that its name came from the Extra Stage ghost Seriko, based on HMX-13 Serio from To Heart.

  3. 2002/06/24 - 伺かゴーストれびゅー そにょ1！ contemporary report

    Contemporary review identifies MIY as developer and artist, describes Seriko and Maruchii's To Heart-derived premise, calls the second shell the scene's first animated ghost, and records Seriko 4 beta 3 as stalled in June 2002.

  4. Seriko 4 preserved package - seriko4040620.zip archived original package

    Package preserved from the creator's es.kdn.jp domain. Internal metadata lists the ghost せりこ, companion まるちい, creator MIY, master shell, scripts, and misaka.dll.

Scope & method
This index covers ghosts first released-or independently documented as existing-from 2000 through 2007. Later updates and official redistributions remain part of those ghosts’ records. Sources accompany each entry, and undocumented details are marked unknown. Packages are preserved only when redistribution permission is documented.
